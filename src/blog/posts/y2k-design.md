The era roughly spanning Dreamcast boot screens, PS2 memory card managers, and
arcade port menus had a visual language all its own: clean white space, candy
colors, pill-shaped everything, and gratuitous orbiting geometry.

## What made it work

It was optimistic. The future was something interfaces were excited about, and
it showed — every menu looked like it was about to take off. Compare that to a
decade of identical gray dashboards and it's not hard to see why the aesthetic
is having a revival.

## Designing inside it

The hard part isn't copying the look; it's knowing what *not* to copy. "INSERT
COIN" makes no sense on a portfolio. The pill buttons, the spinning disc, the
weird little satellite balls? Those translate just fine.
